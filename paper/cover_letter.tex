\opening{Dear Editor,}

We are pleased to submit our original research article entitled
``\textbf{When Does Temperature Scaling Pay Off in Cross-Corpus ECG
Transfer? A Multi-Seed Calibration Boundary Study}'' for your

\emph{Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine}. This manuscript has not
been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by any other
journal.

\bigskip

\textbf{Summary of contributions.}
Temperature scaling (TS) is the default post-hoc recalibration for deep
classifiers, yet whether its in-distribution (ID) benefit transfers to
out-of-distribution (OOD) target corpora remains the central open
question for cross-institution ECG deployment. We pre-registered and
executed a multi-seed calibration boundary study comprising
\textbf{60 experiments} (6 directed transfer pairs $\times$ 2
architectures $\times$ 5 seeds, fully balanced) on PTB-XL,
Chapman-Shaoxing, and CPSC2018, with a patient-level cluster paired
bootstrap (the operating CI is BCa at $B=10{,}000$, fully re-run on the
entire 60-experiment grid) and eight recalibration methods. TS yields a
positive OOD calibration benefit
$\Delta\text{ECE}_{\text{OOD}}=\text{ECE}_{raw}^{OOD}
-\text{ECE}_{TS}^{OOD}$ in \textbf{45/60 = 75.0\%} of seed experiments
(InceptionTime 24/30, ResNet1D 21/30), while the remaining
\textbf{15 counter-examples are transparently disclosed} and
mechanistically explained case by case (seven co-occur with low
OOD accuracy and a large ID--OOD accuracy drop; the remainder are
seed-level temperature mis-transfers, one of which is
over-correction on an already well-calibrated backbone). The
paper delivers \textbf{five independent contributions}:
\textbf{C1} (Primary, OOD benefit quantification: 45/60 seed support),
\textbf{C2} (Boundary condition, pre-registered branch triggered: only
30/60 = 50.0\% of cells have BCa 95\% CIs containing zero, below the
$\geq$80\% criterion, so the ``ID nonzero boundary'' branch applies and
C1 is reported with this qualifier),
\textbf{C3} (Methodological, Shapley decomposition validation: 27/27
cells pass at $n=20{,}000$),
\textbf{C4} (Practical, method-selection boundary: TS the most
consistent recalibrator \emph{among the eight delivered methods} vs.\
EM prior, which is negative on average,
and Matrix scaling, governed by a prior-likelihood
mismatch mechanism), and
\textbf{C5} (Clinical, discrimination-aware calibration gate: 5/12
pair$\times$architecture strata pass both layers under the per-seed
majority criterion, reframing the counter-examples as fifteen Layer-1
rejections, seven of which also fail the discrimination layer).

\bigskip

\textbf{Why this journal.}
Cross-corpus ECG transfer calibration is a critical bottleneck for
clinical deployment of deep ECG models across institutions with
different acquisition protocols, lead sets, and label vocabularies.
Our contribution is evaluated end-to-end on the raw 12-lead ECG
signal: deep classifiers trained on one corpus are pushed through an
eval-time shift ladder (sampling rate, lead availability, noise, gain)
and a six-direction cross-corpus transfer grid, and the contribution is
a boundary-condition characterization of when post-hoc recalibration
pays off at deployment---combined with transparent counter-example
reporting, a discrimination-aware deployment gate, and a
pre-registered failure branch. We believe this falls squarely within
the journal's scope on biomedical signal processing, reliability and
calibration of clinical biosignal models, and reproducible evaluation
methodology.

\textbf{Transparency statement.}
All hypotheses in this manuscript are tagged
\emph{exploratory + robustness validation}, not confirmatory,
consistent with the open-science best practices of
Nosek et al.\ (2018). The fifteen counter-examples (25\% of the
experiment grid) are disclosed in full in the main
post-hoc excluded. Four pre-registration amendments are recorded with
reason and date: A1 re-locates the primary endpoint (protocol
v2.0 $\rightarrow$ v2.1-A1), A2 reduces the confirmatory family
from 156 to 12 hypotheses, re-designating the L2 shift levels as
exploratory dose-response, A3 corrects the identity of the third
corpus (the registered ``CPSC2018+2019'' is in fact \emph{CPSC2018
only} -- the CPSC Database plus the CPSC-Extra Database) and discloses
that CPSC2018's official nine-class taxonomy contains no MI, so the MI
class of our four-class subspace is populated entirely by CPSC-Extra
SNOMED annotations, and A4 (2026-10-11) re-operationalizes the
protocol's registered mapping-sensitivity gate on the delivered primary
endpoint: the registered wording conditioned the gate on the secondary
endpoint $G$, which its own Limitations acknowledge as undelivered on
real data, so the gate now reads cross-pair $\Delta\text{ECE}_{\text{OOD}}$
ordering (Kendall $\tau\geq0.8$) with the confirmatory conclusions
unchanged, defined \emph{before} any mapping-variant result exists. A3
changes no recorded value (naming, attribution and disclosure); A4
changes no recorded value (gate operationalization only; the variant
battery remains a queued deliverable). The protocol and the
amendments are archived as an immutable record with per-file
SHA-256 checksums at \url{https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/53H64}
(amendments A3 and A4 are deposited in the OSF project deposit --
project 53h64 Files, 2026-10-11, per-file SHA-256 verified; the frozen
registration's update draft to v2 is authored and pending submission).
A five-item honesty declaration (adversarial audit product) is
included in the manuscript.
