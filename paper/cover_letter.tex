\opening{Dear Editor,}

We are pleased to submit our original research article entitled
``\textbf{When Does Temperature Scaling Pay Off in Cross-Corpus ECG
Transfer? A Multi-Seed Calibration Boundary Study}'' for your

\bigskip

\textbf{Summary of contributions.}
Temperature scaling (TS) is the default post-hoc recalibration for deep
classifiers, yet whether its in-distribution (ID) benefit transfers to
out-of-distribution (OOD) target corpora remains the central open
question for cross-institution ECG deployment. We pre-registered and
executed a multi-seed calibration boundary study comprising
\textbf{60 experiments} (6 directed transfer pairs $\times$ 2
architectures $\times$ 5 seeds, fully balanced) on PTB-XL,
Chapman-Shaoxing, and CPSC2018+2019, with a patient-level cluster paired
bootstrap (the operating CI is BCa at $B=10{,}000$, fully re-run on the
entire 60-experiment grid) and eight recalibration methods. TS yields a
positive OOD calibration benefit
$\Delta\text{ECE}_{\text{OOD}}=\text{ECE}_{raw}^{OOD}
-\text{ECE}_{TS}^{OOD}$ in \textbf{51/60 = 85.0\%} of seed experiments
(InceptionTime 27/30, ResNet1D 24/30), while the remaining
\textbf{9 counter-examples are transparently disclosed} and
mechanistically explained case by case (six of nine co-occur with low
OOD accuracy; seven of nine with a large ID--OOD accuracy gap). The
paper delivers \textbf{five independent contributions}:
\textbf{C1} (Primary, OOD benefit quantification: 51/60 seed support),
\textbf{C2} (Boundary condition, pre-registered branch triggered: only
23/60 = 38.3\% of cells have BCa 95\% CIs containing zero, below the
$\geq$80\% criterion, so the ``ID nonzero boundary'' branch applies and
C1 is reported with this qualifier),
\textbf{C3} (Methodological, Shapley decomposition validation: 27/27
cells pass at $n=20{,}000$),
\textbf{C4} (Practical, method-selection boundary: TS robust vs.\ EM
prior and Matrix scaling weakest, governed by a prior-likelihood
mismatch mechanism), and
\textbf{C5} (Clinical, discrimination-aware calibration gate: 7/14
strata pass both layers, decoupling calibration effectiveness from
clinical deployability).

\bigskip

\textbf{Why this journal.}
Cross-corpus ECG transfer calibration is a critical bottleneck for
clinical deployment of deep ECG models across institutions with
different acquisition protocols, lead sets, and label vocabularies.
Our methodological contribution---an explicit boundary-condition
characterization of when TS pays off, combined with transparent
reporting of nine counter-examples and a pre-registered failure
branch---aligns directly with the journal's scope on biomedical
signal processing, clinical machine learning reliability, and
reproducible computational methods in medicine.

\bigskip

\textbf{Transparency statement.}
All hypotheses in this manuscript are tagged
\emph{exploratory + robustness validation}, not confirmatory,
consistent with the open-science best practices of
Nosek et al.\ (2018). The nine counter-examples (15.0\% of the
experiment grid) are disclosed in full in the main
text and supplements; no non-supporting result is omitted or
post-hoc excluded. Two pre-registration amendments are recorded with
reason and date: A1 re-locates the primary endpoint (protocol
v2.0 $\rightarrow$ v2.1-A1) and A2 reduces the confirmatory family
from 156 to 12 hypotheses, re-designating the L2 shift levels as
exploratory dose-response. The protocol, the revision documents, and
the file-level hash manifest are included with the submission as
supplementary files; the manifest records the snapshot taken on
2026-09-05. \textbf{Public OSF archival is not yet complete} and is
declared as pending in the manuscript; we will register the protocol and
both amendments, together with an archival-status report, under a public
OSF timestamp before publication.
A five-item honesty declaration (adversarial audit product) is
included in the manuscript.
